Business & Impact

AlzAware is designed as a low-cost digital triage platform that helps older adults, families, clinics, and senior-care organizations recognize when professional cognitive evaluation may be worth considering.

The Challenge

Alzheimer's disease is often recognized after years of subtle cognitive changes . Formal evaluation is important, but requires costly testing.

For many families, knowing when to get tested is crucial.

The opportunity

A low-cost digital reference tool can support earlier conversations, help prioritize limited clinical resources, and guide people toward appropriate professional evaluation.

A scalable first step before higher-cost evaluation

AlzAware creates a low-friction triage layer. Users answer simple health and demographic questions, receive a model-based reference score, and see how that score compares with AD and non-AD study samples.

1

Accessible

Runs through a browser-based assessment and requires no specialized equipment.

2

Interpretable

Compares each result with reference score distributions instead of presenting an unsupported diagnosis.

3

Scalable

Can support families, clinics, senior-care facilities, and community health programs at very low cost.

Who pays and who benefits

The strongest early business path is B2B: serving organizations that already support older adults and need practical tools for cognitive risk awareness.

Senior care

Resident screening

Facilities could subscribe per resident or per assessment to support routine cognitive risk check-ins and family communication.

Clinics

Front-end triage

Primary-care clinics could use AlzAware to help identify patients who may need cognitive screening, specialist referral, or closer monitoring.

Research

Future partnerships

Research groups and clinical-trial teams could use responsible pre-screening and longitudinal tracking to identify people for follow-up studies.

Economic potential

Dementia care represents a multi-hundred-billion-dollar annual burden in the United States. If a low-cost triage layer helped reduce even 1% of avoidable delays, inefficient navigation, or unnecessary friction against the projected 2026 cost burden, the implied system-level value would be roughly $4 billion.

It shows why scalable early-risk triage has meaningful public-health and economic potential.

$3.6M

Illustrative subscription scenario

A $3 per resident per month subscription across 100,000 monitored users would represent about $3.6M in annual recurring revenue.

From prototype to pilots

The next step is not immediate medical deployment. It is careful validation of usability, interpretation, and responsible follow-up workflows.

1

Prototype validation

Use the website, assessment page, and demo video to test whether users understand the score, comparison language, and medical disclaimer.

2

Senior-care and community pilots

Partner with senior-care facilities, caregiver groups, and community health programs to evaluate whether AlzAware improves early conversations and follow-up decisions.

3

Clinic and longitudinal expansion

Expand into primary-care settings and develop repeat assessments that track changes over time while preserving the boundary between risk awareness and diagnosis.
