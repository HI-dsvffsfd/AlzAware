Business & impact

Turning early cognitive risk awareness into scalable public health value

AlzAware is designed as a low-cost digital triage platform that helps older adults, families, clinics, and senior-care organizations identify when professional cognitive evaluation may be worth considering.

The problem

Formal diagnosis is important, but first-step access is limited

Alzheimer's disease is progressive and difficult to predict because risk is shaped by age, health history, cognitive performance, genetics, and many other factors.

Current evaluation may involve cognitive testing, blood tests, brain imaging, amyloid PET scans, or spinal fluid testing. These tools are clinically important, but they can be expensive, time-consuming, and inaccessible for many older adults.

The opportunity

A low-cost digital risk reference tool can help people decide when to seek formal evaluation, while helping clinics and care organizations prioritize limited clinical resources.

Technology

How AlzAware works

The prototype uses questionnaire-based health information and a trained XGBoost model to generate a cognitive risk reference score. The result is then compared with AD and non-AD populations from a large reference dataset.

1

Collect

Users enter age, education, medication count, and self-reported medical history through a short digital assessment.

2

Score

The machine-learning model produces a reference score based on processed predictors from the training dataset.

3

Compare

The output shows where the user's score falls compared with AD and non-AD reference groups.

Model performance

A strong AUC supports screening potential

ROC-AUC measures how well a model separates AD and non-AD participants across possible thresholds. A value closer to 1.0 indicates stronger discrimination.

0.89

AlzAware's current XGBoost prototype reaches an approximate testing AUC of 0.89, suggesting strong potential as a first-step risk triage tool.

Final reported values should be updated with the exact training run, confidence interval, and model version used in the final submission.

Reference distributions make the result interpretable

This is useful because it communicates both model separation and uncertainty: AD participants tend to receive higher scores, but overlap remains. That is why AlzAware is positioned as risk triage, not diagnosis.

Who benefits

Economic value across families, clinics, and care systems

AlzAware's value is not limited to individual users. At scale, early risk awareness can support better care navigation, reduce delayed evaluation, and help organizations use clinical resources more efficiently.

Older adults and families

Families can use a simple first-step assessment to decide whether memory or attention concerns deserve professional follow-up, reducing uncertainty and helping conversations start earlier.

Primary-care clinics

Clinics can use a fast digital triage layer to prioritize patients who may need cognitive screening, specialist referral, or closer monitoring.

Senior-care facilities

Care organizations can run repeatable check-ins for residents and track risk signals over time, supporting earlier family and clinician communication.

Health systems and insurers

A scalable triage tool could help reduce inefficient care pathways by guiding high-risk users toward evaluation while reassuring lower-risk users that routine follow-up may be enough.

Economic potential

Small efficiency gains can represent large system-level value

Dementia care is already a multi-hundred-billion-dollar annual burden in the United States. If a low-cost digital triage layer helped reduce even 1% of avoidable friction, delayed evaluation, or inefficient care navigation against the projected 2026 cost burden, the implied system-level value would be roughly $4 billion.

This is an illustrative scenario, not a guaranteed saving. It shows why scalable early-risk triage has meaningful economic potential.

$3.6M

Illustrative subscription scenario

A $3 per resident per month subscription across 100,000 monitored users would represent about $3.6M in annual recurring revenue.

Business model

A B2B platform with public-health reach

The strongest early business path is not selling diagnosis to individuals. It is offering a responsible risk-triage platform to organizations that already support older adults.

Senior-care subscriptions

Facilities pay per resident or per assessment to support routine cognitive risk awareness and family communication.

Clinic licensing

Primary-care clinics license the tool as a front-end triage workflow before formal cognitive screening or referral.

Research partnerships

Future partnerships could support clinical-trial pre-screening, longitudinal monitoring, and anonymized model improvement.

Customer acquisition strategy

From prototype to scalable pilots

1

Prototype validation

Use the website, assessment page, and demo video to validate messaging, usability, result interpretation, and responsible medical boundaries.

2

Senior-care and community pilots

Partner with senior-care facilities, caregiver groups, and community health programs to test whether AlzAware improves early conversations and follow-up decisions.

3

Clinic integration

Expand into primary-care settings where clinicians need efficient tools to prioritize cognitive screening and specialist referrals.

4

Longitudinal monitoring

Develop monthly or quarterly check-ins that track score changes over time, making subtle cognitive risk signals easier to notice.

Mission, vision, values

Responsible innovation is part of the product

AlzAware is built around a clear medical boundary. It supports awareness, triage, and follow-up conversations, but it does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent Alzheimer's disease.

Mission

Make early cognitive risk awareness easier to access through a simple digital assessment and interpretable model output.

Vision

Create a scalable first-step triage layer for families, clinics, senior-care organizations, and community health programs.

Values

Accessibility, transparency, privacy, responsible AI, and respect for the difference between risk awareness and diagnosis.
