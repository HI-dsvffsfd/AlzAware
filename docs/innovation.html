Innovation overview

Digital triage for earlier Alzheimer’s risk awareness

AlzAware transforms self-reported health information into an interpretable reference score, helping users understand relative cognitive risk before deciding whether to seek formal evaluation.

1

Questionnaire

Age, education, medications, and self-reported medical history.

2

Model score

A trained machine-learning model estimates a risk-reference score.

3

Reference comparison

The score is compared with AD and non-AD populations.

4

Next step

Users receive responsible, educational guidance to consider professional follow-up.

The problem

Early cognitive changes are often noticed too late

Formal cognitive evaluation can be expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to access, especially for rural communities, under-resourced clinics, and senior-care facilities. As a result, many people wait until symptoms interfere with daily life before seeking help.

The gap

Healthcare systems need a low-cost, scalable way to support early risk awareness without overclaiming diagnosis or replacing professional assessment.

Our solution

A reference-based score that users can understand

AlzAware does not simply tell users a raw probability. It contextualizes the score by showing how many people with AD and without AD in a large reference dataset had higher scores.

This makes the output easier to interpret and safer for public communication: the result becomes a comparative risk-awareness signal, not a diagnosis.
